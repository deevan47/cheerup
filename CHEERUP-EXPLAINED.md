# Cheer Up, explained

Everything I understand about this business, written out so you can check it.
If a line here is wrong, that is a line I have been building the website on.

Where something is still undecided, it says so rather than guessing. Those are
collected at the end, in section 12.

---

## 1. The idea in one line

**You know that quiet second after someone opens a gift and realises it was made
for them? We make that.**

The product is a mug, a print, a keychain. The thing being sold is the proof that
somebody was paying attention.

---

## 2. The thought behind it

Most gifting is a transaction that both people quietly understand. Something gets
bought, something gets unwrapped, a thank you is exchanged, and nobody is moved.
The gift was a placeholder for the thought, not evidence of it.

Cheer Up is built on the opposite bet: that the reaction people actually want is
not gratitude but a **pause**. The half-second where the person opening it goes
quiet, looks again, and understands that this could not have been bought for
anyone else. Their photo. Their name. The joke only the two of you share.

Two things follow from that, and they shape every decision in the business:

1. **It cannot be stocked.** A gift that proves attention has to be made after
   the order, for one named person. There is no shelf, no inventory, nothing
   that could have gone to somebody else.
2. **The buyer is nervous.** They are not buying a known object, they are
   commissioning something they have not seen, for someone whose reaction
   matters to them. So the entire process is built to remove that fear rather
   than to process a payment quickly.

The brand line is **"Smile Delivered."** The business is one person in Pune:
**Deevan**.

## 4. The fourteenth category: anything else

There is a **Request a gift** page, and it is not an afterthought. It accepts
five kinds of request:

1. A new product
2. A gift for a specific need
3. A bulk or team order
4. A change to something you already make
5. A suggestion

This matters more than it looks. It converts the catalogue from a limit into a
starting point, and it means a visitor who does not find what they want has
somewhere to go other than away. It is also how the catalogue grows: the
requests tell you what to add next, paid for by the person asking.

## 5. Who the customer is

There are two people in every order, and they are not the same person.

**The buyer** is someone in Pune with an occasion coming and a feeling they
cannot articulate. Often they do not know what to give. The site is built around
that: one of the eight options on the home page is literally **"Not sure yet"**,
and choosing it does not show products, it offers a conversation.

The eight relationships the site is organised around:

**Mum · Dad · Partner · Best friend · Sibling · Colleague · Teacher · Not sure yet**

**The recipient** never visits the site and never pays. They are the one the
business is actually judged by, because the whole proposition is their reaction.

Everything is written to the buyer about the recipient. That is why the copy
says *their* photo, *their* name, *your* inside joke.

## 6. How an order works, start to finish

Seven steps. **Four of them are the customer's, three are yours.** The website
marks which is which, because "seven steps" on its own makes the process sound
heavier than it is.

| # | Step | Whose | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell me who it's for | **Customer** | A photo, a name, a joke, or just "help, it's their birthday." They give the feeling; you find the gift. |
| 2 | See it, free | Yours | You design it and send a preview. Free, no strings, no commitment. |
| 3 | Make it feel just right | **Customer** | They change anything they want. Nothing prints until they say yes. |
| 4 | Say yes, pay 70% | **Customer** | On approval only. It then goes to print with a trusted Pune vendor. |
| 5 | I check it, by hand | Yours | Checked against the approved design. If it is not right, it does not leave. |
| 6 | At their door in 24 hours | Yours | Hand-delivered across Pune, with WhatsApp updates throughout. |
| 7 | Pay the last 30% at the door | **Customer** | Only once it is in their hands. Then the photos are deleted for good. |

All of it happens in one WhatsApp thread. There is no account, no cart, no
checkout, no app.

## 7. The money

**70% after the customer approves the design. 30% on delivery.**

No online payment of any kind. It is settled in the chat and at the door.

Read as a business decision rather than a payment method, this does three things:

- **It removes the buyer's risk entirely at the point where they feel it most.**
  They have seen the exact thing they are buying before any money moves. The
  free preview is the single most important asset in the funnel, and it costs
  only your time.
- **It protects you from the real risk of made-to-order work**, which is paying
  a printer for something a customer then abandons. The 70% lands before print.
- **The last 30% is a quality bond.** You are paid in full only after they are
  holding it. That is a promise you can make precisely because you check and
  deliver each one yourself.

The working capital requirement is close to zero. You hold no stock, you print
only against a paid order, and the customer funds the print run.

## 8. Why WhatsApp, and why that is the model, not a shortcut

Orders are placed and confirmed over WhatsApp. Every button on the site opens a
chat with a message already written for the context it was pressed in: a general
order, a specific product, a gift idea for a specific relationship, a custom
request, a question, or a photo of a delivered gift.

This is not a cheaper substitute for a store. It is the correct shape for what
is being sold:

- A buyer who does not know what they want **cannot be served by a product grid**.
  They need to be asked questions. A chat does that; a checkout cannot.
- The preview-and-revise loop in steps 2 and 3 is a conversation by nature.
- In India, WhatsApp is where people already are. No install, no signup, no
  friction between wanting and asking.
- It makes the one-person structure a feature. The person replying is the person
  making the gift, which is exactly the thing an app cannot offer.

The cost is that it does not scale without you. Every order needs your attention.
That is a real ceiling and it is worth naming (section 12).

## 9. The positioning: one person beats an app

The stated reasons, in your own framing:

1. **One human, start to finish.** The person who reads the message is the person
   who makes the gift.
2. **Made for one, never mass-made.** Nothing off a shelf.
3. **Checked by hand, every time.** Against the approved design. Not right, does
   not go.
4. **Clueless? Perfect.** Not knowing what to give is treated as the normal
   starting point, not a problem.

The competitive claim is not that you are cheaper or faster than a printing
marketplace. It is that a marketplace cannot care, and caring is the product.
"No call centre. No warehouse. Just someone who truly cares."

Cheer Up is small **on purpose**, so each gift gets full attention.

## 11. Trust, privacy and the rules

**What is collected:** name, phone number, delivery address, and whatever photo
or text is sent for personalisation. Nothing else.

**What it is used for:** designing, printing and delivering that one gift. It is
shared only with the vendor who prints the order. It is never sold and never
shared with anyone else.

**What happens afterwards:** after delivery, the photos and details are deleted
from your phone, your files, and the WhatsApp chat. No copies kept. The site also
states plainly that WhatsApp is run by Meta and that Meta's own privacy policy
covers messages sent through it.

This matters more than a normal privacy policy, because customers are sending
photographs of their families, their partners, and their children to a stranger's
personal phone. The deletion promise is a sales argument, not boilerplate.

**Cancellation and refunds**

- Cancel before the design is approved and printing begins: **full refund of the
  advance.**
- Once printing has started: **no cancellation, no refund**, because it is made
  for one person and nobody else can use it.
- Arrives damaged or different from the approved design: tell you within **24
  hours with a photo**, and you reprint or refund.

**Delivery**

Pune only, for now. Printed, quality-checked and hand-delivered, usually within
24 hours of approval and advance payment, with WhatsApp updates at every step.

**Terms**

Run by a single founder in Pune. All orders placed and confirmed over WhatsApp.
Production begins only after a free preview is approved and 70% is paid. Prices
and delivery times may change without notice. You reserve the right to decline an
order you cannot fulfil to your quality standard.

## 12. What is not settled yet

These are genuine blanks in the business, not missing website copy. Each one is
marked in the code as needing you.

**Commercial**

- **Prices for most of the range.** Set so far: classic, colour-inside, heart
  holder and magic mugs; both Polaroids; both phone cases; the heart keychain;
  the T-shirt. Everything else shows "Price on request" until a figure is set in
  `src/data/products.js`.
- **The delivery charge.** Not decided. It is the question buyers ask most and
  the one thing the Delivery page cannot answer. Free or a figure, it needs to
  exist.
- **Whether Polaroids are really priced per print.** The site says ₹35 and ₹45
  per print. If they are sold in packs, that is wrong and it is the cheapest,
  most-seen price on the site.

**Legal**

- **The trading or legal name** the Terms contract under. Right now a buyer
  cannot tell who they are entering an agreement with.
- **The jurisdiction** whose courts settle a dispute.
- A lawyer should read the Privacy and Terms pages. Both make promises you can be
  held to, particularly the deletion promise.

**Proof**

- **There are no reviews.** Not one. The page previously carried three invented
  ones labelled as samples, which is worse than none, so they were removed and
  Reviews was taken out of the navigation until there are at least three real
  ones with permission.
- **No photographs of delivered gifts**, for the same reason.

**Product photography**

- **Every product on the site currently has an empty image space**, on purpose,
  waiting for your photos. Each one has a comment in `src/data/products.js`
  naming the file to add. The ten older photos were not switched back on because
  they show **the Cheer Up logo printed on the product**: the proposition is
  "their photo, their name, your inside joke", and those images show none of it.
  New photos should show a sample photo or name on the product.
- **There is no photograph of you.** The page that sells "a real person who
  cares" has no face on it.

**Structural**

- The model does not scale past your own hours. Every order needs you for
  design, checking and delivery. Growth either means longer days, or changing one
  of the three promises that make the business what it is.
- Pune only. Deliberate for now, and the 24-hour promise depends on it.

## 15. The whole thing, compressed

A one-person business in Pune that makes personalised gifts to order and
hand-delivers them within 24 hours, selling entirely through WhatsApp
conversations rather than a checkout, charging from ₹35, taking no money until
the customer has seen and approved a free design, taking the last 30% only once
the gift is in their hands, and deleting their photos afterwards.

It holds no stock, needs almost no capital, and competes on the one thing a
marketplace structurally cannot copy: a person who actually cares how it lands.

What it is really selling is not the object. It is the moment the object creates,
and the evidence it leaves behind that somebody was thinking of you.
